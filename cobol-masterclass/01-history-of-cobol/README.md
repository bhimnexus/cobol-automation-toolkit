# History of COBOL

---

## 1. Background Before COBOL

During the 1950s, computers were increasingly used for business data processing.  
However, each computer manufacturer provided its own programming language.

As a result:
- Programs written for one computer could not run on another
- Business organizations faced difficulty maintaining programs
- Rewriting programs for different systems was costly and time-consuming

At the same time, business applications such as payroll, accounting, and inventory systems were growing rapidly.

There was a strong need for a **common, standardized business-oriented programming language**.

---

## 2. Formation of CODASYL

To address this need, a committee called:

**CODASYL**  
(Conference on Data Systems Languages)

was formed in **1959**.

### Objective of CODASYL:
- To develop a common programming language
- To support business data processing
- To enable portability across different computer systems

---

## 3. Development of COBOL

COBOL was developed by the CODASYL committee in **1959**.

The **first COBOL compiler** was released in **December 1959**.

---

## 4. Meaning of COBOL

The word **COBOL** is an acronym for:

**COmmon Business Oriented Language**

Each part of the name reflects its purpose:
- **Common** — Designed to run on different computer systems
- **Business Oriented** — Designed for business applications
- **Language** — A formal programming language with defined syntax

---

## 5. Purpose of COBOL

COBOL was designed to:
- Handle large volumes of business data
- Support file-oriented processing
- Generate reports and summaries
- Be readable and easy to understand

COBOL programs are primarily used for **application development**, not system software.

---

## 6. What COBOL Is Not Designed For

COBOL is **not intended** for:
- Writing operating systems
- Writing compilers
- Writing system-level utilities
- Hardware control or device drivers

These tasks are better suited to low-level languages.

---

## 7. ANSI and COBOL Standardization

After COBOL was introduced, responsibility for maintaining and enhancing the language was taken over by:

**ANSI**  
(American National Standards Institute)

ANSI ensured:
- Standardization of COBOL syntax
- Portability of programs
- Consistency across vendors

---

## 8. Important COBOL Versions

### 8.1 First ANSI Approved Version — 1968
- First official standardized version of COBOL

---

### 8.2 Modified ANSI Approved Version — 1974
- Known as **OS/VS COBOL**
- Widely used on IBM mainframe systems

---

### 8.3 Modified ANSI Approved Version — 1985
- Known as **VS COBOL II**
- Introduced structured programming features
- Added scope terminators such as END-IF and END-PERFORM

---

## 9. IBM COBOL for OS/390

This course is based on **IBM COBOL for OS/390 (Version 2 Release 2)**.

This version:
- Runs on IBM mainframe operating systems
- Forms the foundation for modern **Enterprise COBOL for z/OS**
- Is widely used in enterprise environments

---

## 10. Continued Relevance of COBOL

COBOL continues to be widely used because:
- Existing systems are stable and reliable
- Business logic is deeply embedded in COBOL programs
- Rewriting legacy systems is expensive and risky

---

## 11. Key Points to Remember

- COBOL was developed in **1959** by **CODASYL**
- First COBOL compiler released in **December 1959**
- ANSI standardized COBOL starting in **1968**
- COBOL is designed for **business application development**
- IBM COBOL evolved into modern **Enterprise COBOL**
